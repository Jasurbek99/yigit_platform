Technical Decision Memo · Prepared for Management

Two Programs, One Decision

Consolidating YGT Platform and Sera Bütçe Yönetimi into a single product for the greenhouse → export business.

Question: which program should absorb the other? Method: live inspection of both running apps + source review Date: 25 July 2026

Recommendation Build on YGT Platform

Keep YGT Platform as the company's system, and rebuild Sera Bütçe's budget & greenhouse strengths as modules inside it. The two apps look alike because they serve the same business — but they are different classes of software, and that asymmetry sets the direction: merging Sera's screens onto YGT's shared backend is a matter of weeks; the reverse would mean rebuilding YGT's foundation from scratch.

YGT Platform

10.10.39.243:3000 · Django + React + MSSQL
  • Multi-user system of record — the whole company works off one shared database.
  • 40+ relational tables, 12 roles, per-role permissions, secure JWT login.
  • Full audit log, validated status lifecycle, ~24,000 lines of automated tests.
  • REST API (ready for a future mobile app); 3 languages (TK / RU / EN).
  • Focus today: the export shipment lifecycle.

Sera Bütçe Yönetimi

10.10.49.54:3001 · React, per-account storage
  • Single-user planning & budgeting tool — each person keeps a private copy of the data.
  • Polished, fast, 42 screens; genuinely strong quota & budget modules.
  • Deep on greenhouse cultivation economics — cost, fertilizer, consumables, salary, packaging.
  • Data saved as one JSON document per account; no roles, audit, or shared source of truth.
  • 2 languages (TR / TM).

1The decisive fact: how each program stores data

This is not a matter of opinion — it is visible directly in each app's own network traffic, and it determines everything else.

In plain terms: Sera is like an excellent personal Excel workbook that lives in the browser; YGT is the shared company database many people safely write to at once. That difference is the entire reason one is faster to build and the other is what the company actually runs on.

2Why the merge goes this direction (and not the other)

Putting Sera's screens on top of YGT's backend is a user-interface and data-modeling job. Doing it the other way — adding shared multi-user data, roles, audit, security and a real API underneath Sera — means rebuilding precisely the hard 80% that YGT Platform already has. The effort is not symmetric.

Already the chosen direction Sera's 42 screens have been captured as reference material inside the YGT repository (sera-ref/), and a build brief exists to port them into the YGT frontend as a "Sera Bütçe" module. The direction is scoped — this memo confirms it is the right one.

3What Sera genuinely brings — real scope, not just a nicer look

Sera is bigger than a dashboard. Its 42 screens cover a whole greenhouse production-economics domain that YGT does not yet model, which is exactly what makes merging worthwhile rather than redundant:

  • Cultivation costs — fertilizer, consumables, production overhead, per-block settings.
  • Budget & planning — plan-vs-actual for production, revenue, cost and profit; weekly production plans.
  • Personnel & salary, marketing/packaging costs, management & finance reports.
  • Greenhouse monitoring — climate, disease, maps.
  • A well-designed quota module (issued vs. used per firm, live balance).

YGT today is strongest on the export shipment lifecycle; Sera is strongest on the greenhouse → budget side. They are largely complementary.

4The real cost and risk — stated plainly

This is not "bolt on a budget page." The dominant cost is reconciling entities both apps model independently:

Overlap that must be reconciled Buyers/firms (Sera's alyjylar — e.g. "Nur-Alem" — vs. YGT's Customer / Export Firm / Import Firm), plus a parallel version of TIR/export, quota, contracts, customs and sales. Sera also holds real accumulated data (~1.1 MB of state + ~400 KB of planting records) that must be migrated into YGT's tables, not just re-keyed.

The rule for the merge: where the two overlap, YGT's model wins (it is the shared, constrained one) and Sera's overlapping screens are re-pointed at YGT's existing data. Where Sera is new (greenhouse cost & budget), it becomes new modules and new tables in YGT.

5A phased path to one product

Phase 1

Sera screens on YGT data — where entities already exist

Rebuild Sera's export/TIR, quota, contracts and sales views on top of YGT's live shared data. Fast wins, no new schema, immediate single-source-of-truth benefit.

Phase 2

New greenhouse-cost & budget tables + data migration

Add the genuinely new domain — cultivation costs, budget plan-vs-actual, personnel/salary, monitoring — as new YGT modules, and migrate Sera's accumulated data into them.

Team

One team, one product

Combine Sera's UI/UX strengths with YGT's secure shared backend. The outcome is one product and one team instead of two half-products and a rivalry.

6The one-line version for the boss

Sera is an excellent planning tool; YGT is the shared operational system a planning tool can't replace. Merge Sera's ideas into YGT — keep YGT as the base, rebuild Sera's budget and greenhouse strengths inside it.
